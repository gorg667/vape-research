# Research notes 05 — Health, harm reduction, and practical usage (for an adult daily user)

## Health Canada position (canada.ca/vaping/risks, updated Jun 4 2026)
- Nicotine → dependence; short-term ↑BP and HR. Nicotine itself not known to cause cancer, respiratory or vascular disease.
- Switching completely from smoking to vaping is less harmful than continuing to smoke; but vaping is NOT harmless.
- Aerosol contains PG, VG, flavourings; at high temps → carbonyls (formaldehyde, acetaldehyde, acrolein).
  **Higher power/temperature settings produce more harmful chemicals** → practical: use Eco/Normal mode, avoid chain-vaping hot coils.
- Metals (nickel, lead) leach from ageing heating elements → levels far lower than cigarette smoke but non-zero.
- Common short-term effects: cough, nausea, headache, throat/mouth irritation.
- Popcorn lung: diacetyl found in only 2 of 800+ Canadian liquids (2017–19); no confirmed Canadian vaping popcorn-lung cases.
- Vape outdoors; second-hand aerosol far lower risk than smoke but nicotine detectable in bystanders.
- 1 in 5 Canadians who quit smoking in 2024 used vaping (Canada Tobacco Strategy).

## UC Davis study (ACS Central Science, June 2025) — disposables & metals
- 7 disposable devices, 3 popular brands (US market, mostly illegal/unregulated 5% devices). 500–1500 puffs each.
- Some released more lead in a day's use than ~20 packs of cigarettes; nickel, antimony exceeded cancer-risk thresholds for 3 devices.
- Sources: leaded-bronze alloy components leaching into e-liquid; nickel from coils; antimony in unused liquid.
- Levels of Cr, Ni, Sb INCREASED with puff count → ageing coil matters. Relevance: the 80K-puff class runs ONE coil for 30 mL. Toss a device
  once it tastes burnt/metallic — don't "finish it". Regulated Canadian product ≠ tested-safe; Health Canada does not pre-approve devices.
- Caveat: only 3 brands, US unregulated market; can't generalize to specific Canadian SKUs. But argues for: (a) buy stamped/regulated,
  (b) prefer brands with long track records, (c) don't run coils to death, (d) pod systems with fresh coil per pod (Loop Max / Level X) arguably better.

## Sucralose (El-Hage et al. 2019, Sci News): heating sucralose-sweetened liquid produces chloropropanols & more aldehydes. Very sweet
  "candy" disposables likely contain sucralose. Lower-sweet profiles (WAKA Cuke Lemon Lime, tobacco, mint) are arguably lower-risk.

## Nicotine math for a daily user (site should include calculator)
- 20 mg/mL × 30 mL = 600 mg nicotine per ultra device. Typical vaping absorbs ~30–50% → still ~200–300 mg absorbed per device.
- A cigarette delivers ~1–1.5 mg absorbed. So one 30 mL device ≈ 150–300 cigarettes' worth ≈ 7–15 packs.
- Finishing a 30 mL in 3 days ≈ 3–5 packs/day equivalent (Dr. Hassan Mir, Ottawa Heart Inst: some vape patients at "100 cigarettes/day" nicotine).
- Practical: track days-per-device. If <7 days on 30 mL, you're at very high intake; consider step-down (Oxbar G42K adjustable nic; Orbito 10 mg;
  refillable pod with 10/6/3 mg salts).

## Practical tips
- Charge with 5V/1A–2A; avoid fast chargers >2A unless device rated (Linvo). Don't charge overnight unattended on a bed.
- Store <30°C; hot cars degrade liquid + swell batteries.
- If liquid pools in mouthpiece: blow out gently into a tissue, wipe, or it will gurgle/spit.
- If it tastes burnt after charging: coil dry — let sit 5 min, take gentle primer puffs. If still burnt → done.
- Airflow: tighter = more MTL/cigarette-like, less juice per puff; open = RDL, burns juice faster.
- Puff modes: Eco for all-day, Boost for a short "hit"; Boost cuts life by 25–40%.
- Carry-on only when flying; never checked baggage (CATSA). Vaping prohibited on flights.
- Quitting/cutting down resources: Smokers' Helpline 1-877-513-5333 / smokershelpline.ca; Health Canada "Quit vaping"; Ontario pharmacist-prescribed NRT
  free under Ontario Pharmacare programs; Ottawa Heart Institute vaping cessation clinic (referral).

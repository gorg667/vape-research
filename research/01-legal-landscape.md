# Research notes 01 — Legal / regulatory landscape (Toronto, Ontario, Canada) — as of Sept 2026

## Federal (Tobacco and Vaping Products Act + regs)
- Nicotine cap: **20 mg/mL max** since July 2021 (Nicotine Concentration in Vaping Products Regulations).
  Anything labelled 50 mg / 5% is NOT a legal Canadian product -> grey/illicit import.
  Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/smoking-tobacco/vaping.html
- Federal excise duty (Excise Act 2001, Sched. 8), since Oct 2022 (stamps mandatory on all product Jan 2023):
  - $1.12 per 2 mL (or fraction) for first 10 mL; $1.12 per 10 mL after (rates since July 1 2024).
  - Ontario is a "specified vaping province" (since July 1 2024) => ADDITIONAL provincial duty at the SAME rate.
  - Net effect in Ontario: **$2.24 per 2 mL** for first 10 mL. A 20 mL disposable = 5×2.24 + 1×2.24 (10-20 mL bracket) = **$13.44 duty**; 10 mL = $11.20; 2 mL = $2.24. Plus 13% HST on top.
  - Legit product carries an excise stamp (Ontario-specific stamp "ON" peach/orange colour + federal). NO STAMP = illegal product.
  Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/excise-taxes-duties-and-levies/excise-duty-vaping-products/calculating-vaping-duty.html
- Federal flavour ban (tobacco/mint/menthol only): announced June 2021, regs drafted, STILL NOT IN FORCE as of May–June 2026.
  Health Minister Marjorie Michel "willing to move in that direction", no timeline. CBC May 10 2026:
  https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/vaping-flavour-ban-federal-government-marjorie-michel-may-2026-9.7190735
  Provinces WITH flavour bans: Quebec, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, PEI. Ontario does NOT (yet).
- Health Canada compliance report Apr 2024–Mar 2025 (published Feb 2026): 546 specialty vape shops inspected,
  **43% non-compliant**, seizures at 235 shops; 286,764 non-compliant products seized. Convenience stores ~1% violation.
  Common violations: >20 mg/mL nicotine, missing health warnings, illegal flavour promotion, no excise stamps.
  https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/smoking-tobacco/vaping/compliance-enforcement/report-april-2024-march-2025.html
- Health Canada also requires: child-resistant packaging, nicotine concentration on label, health warnings, no lifestyle ads.
- Precedent: flavoured nicotine pouches restricted to pharmacies (Aug 2024) via ministerial order — shows Ottawa CAN act fast.

## Ontario (Smoke-Free Ontario Act, 2017)
- Legal age **19+**; ID anyone who looks under 25 (standard practice).
- Flavours: since July 1 2020, only tobacco / mint / menthol may be sold at general retail (convenience, gas).
  ALL other flavours (fruit, dessert, drink, ice) => **specialty vape stores only** (and licensed cannabis stores, in theory).
- Specialty vape store = registers with local public health unit (Toronto Public Health), primarily sells vape
  products, 19+ entry only, may display products & allow viewing. Toronto registration page:
  https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/health-wellness-care/health-inspections-monitoring/smoking-legislation-enforcement/smoke-free-ontario-act-2017/specialty-vape-store-registration/
- General retailers must keep products out of sight; no handling before purchase.
- Ontario provincial vape tax = participates in federal "additional duty" regime (since July 1 2024) — doubles duty.
- Where you can't vape: enclosed public places, workplaces, patios, school grounds, within 20 m of playgrounds/sports
  fields, etc. Toronto municipal by-laws add to this.
- **Bill 125 (NDP, France Gélinas et al., tabled May 26 2026)** "Smoke-Free Ontario Amendment Act (Vaping is not for Kids), 2026":
  would raise age to 21, ban ALL non-tobacco flavours, ban online sales, require board-of-health approval for
  vape shops, ban all promotion. Private member's bill; first reading only; has been tabled before (Bill 103, 2023)
  and died. Low probability of passing under PC majority but signals direction. https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-44/session-1/bill-125
- Ontario also has separate rule: Cannabis stores may sell vapes? (AGCO — actually not nicotine; ignore.)

## Practical implications for a Toronto buyer
1. Legit disposables in Toronto are ALL 20 mg/mL (2%) or lower. Anything "5%" is illicit.
2. Fruit/ice/dessert flavours ONLY at registered specialty vape shops. Convenience stores: tobacco/mint/menthol only
   (e.g., Vuse Go in mint/menthol, STLTH tobacco etc.).
3. Check for excise stamp on the box (federal + ON). No stamp = unregulated import — unknown nicotine, no QA, no Health Canada
   reporting, funding illicit trade, and shop is breaking the law (43% do).
4. Prices are HIGH vs US: duty alone is $11.20–13.44 on a 10–20 mL disposable, before markup and HST. Expect $25–45 for large disposables.
5. Online sales legal in Ontario (age-verified courier). Websites are sparse/age-gated because of federal promo rules.
6. Regulatory risk: a federal or Ontario flavour ban could land with little warning. Don't stockpile huge quantities
   (e-liquid degrades, batteries self-discharge), but be aware.
